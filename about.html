About

Gameplay and systems designer focused on how mechanics shape meaning, behavior, and long-term player engagement.

Who I Am

I’m a gameplay and systems designer who cares about how things feel, how they behave over time, and what they quietly say to the player. My work spans narrative JRPGs, satirical idle games, mobile social apps, and prototypes that exist purely to explore an idea.

I’m especially interested in the point where systems, narrative, and player psychology overlap — how a loop can suggest a worldview, how constraints can become expressive, and how “fun” and “thoughtful” don’t have to be opposites.

Design Philosophy

Across projects, I approach design with a systems-first mindset:

  • Systems as language — Mechanics should communicate ideas, not just carry numbers.
  • Player behavior over power — I care less about how big the numbers get, and more about what players choose to do.
  • Intentional constraint — Limits (no grinding, fixed routes, simple resources) create clearer, more expressive decisions.
  • Ethical engagement — Retention and monetization should feel respectful, not extractive.
  • Scalability — Systems should survive growth, new content, and real-world usage without collapsing.

What I Work On

A few projects that represent how I think and build:

  • Past the End — A narrative JRPG where handcrafted encounters, exploration-driven progression, and absurdist philosophy all work together to explore what “meaningful choice” looks like when the ending doesn’t change.
  • Cult of Coin — A Unity idle game about compulsion, progression systems, and how player agency can quietly erode inside efficient economies — all wrapped in a silly, satirical tone.
  • Song of the Day — A cozy social music app built with Expo and Firebase, focused on calm UX, privacy-aware design, and scalable social features centered on one simple daily ritual.
  • Emberrun & Trailflip — Prototypes that pushed my understanding of scope, mobile-first design, and how to build interesting systems from a small, approachable set of mechanics.

Whether I’m working on a JRPG encounter, an idle economy, or a social feature, the throughline is the same: use systems to say something, and make sure the player can feel it.
